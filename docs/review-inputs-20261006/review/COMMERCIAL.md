## 2. Dataset register

The question underneath every row: can a dataset licensed for non-commercial use be used for evaluation only, never training, during development of a commercial product?

**Answer: not safely, and for the Meta research licences, explicitly not.** CC BY-NC 4.0 defines "NonCommercial" as "not primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation" [V: https://spdx.org/licenses/CC-BY-NC-4.0 §1]. Creative Commons' own interpretation: "The definition of NonCommercial depends on the primary purpose for which the work is used, not on the category or class of reuser"; "it is only the primary purpose of the reuse that needs to be considered" [V: https://wiki.creativecommons.org/NonCommercial_interpretation]. There is no evaluation carve-out and no research carve-out: a company benchmarking a component it intends to ship is using the footage for a purpose directed towards commercial advantage. The fact that the model's weights never touch the footage does not change the purpose of the use. Bristol sells a perpetual commercial licence for exactly this (£7,000 for organisations of five or fewer) [V: https://express-licences.bristol.ac.uk/product/epic-kitchens-100---dataset], which is itself evidence that the licensor does not regard company-internal use as NonCommercial. Meta's Aria Digital Twin licence removes any doubt for its family: "you may not use the Dataset to develop any product that is currently (or that you intend to make or that becomes) available for any commercial use" [V: https://www.projectaria.com/datasets/adt/license/ §1(a)]. Evaluation during development is development.

What is defensible: a published, non-product academic comparison (a paper) can cite NC benchmarks; counsel may accept a narrow "we ran a public benchmark once to report a number in a paper" position. What is not: NC footage in the repo's test fixtures, in CI, or in the week-1/week-4 decisions that choose what the product trains on. `data/epic/P02_102.MP4` and `blockers/eval_epic.py` [V: code/perception/memory_pipeline.py L3; code/CLAUDE.md L536, L554] fall on the wrong side today.

## 4. Regulatory and privacy constraints the build must respect

### 4.1 FDA: general wellness vs device

- The January 6, 2026 "General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices" guidance (superseding the 2019 version) keeps two intended-use categories: "(1) an intended use that relates to maintaining or encouraging a general state of health or a healthy activity" and "(2) an intended use that relates the role of healthy lifestyle with helping to reduce the risk or impact of certain chronic diseases or conditions"; category 1 examples include "mental acuity" ("improve mental acuity, instruction following, concentration, problem-solving ..."); the product must be non-invasive, not implanted, and not involve technology that poses safety risks without regulatory controls; the guidance has no example about memory, dementia or Alzheimer's [V: https://www.fda.gov/media/90652/download].
- The September 28, 2022 "Policy for Device Software Functions and Mobile Medical Applications" guidance lists, under enforcement discretion, "Software functions that keep track of medications and provide user-configured reminders for improved medication adherence" (Appendix B, example 11) and, as not devices, "Software functions that provide patients with simple tools to organize and record their health information" (Appendix A §12); it also names "medication reminders – Product code NXQ" as devices "intended for medical purposes to provide alerts to patients or health care providers for pre-determined medication dosing schedules" [V: https://www.fda.gov/media/80958/download].

What this means for PAM:

1. **Where the medication features sit.** "Where are my pills" is object location; the medication is incidental to the function (the same function finds glasses and keys). That is category-1 general wellness or, at most, an Appendix A §12 organiser, provided the labeling says "find your belongings" and not "manage your dementia." A disease name in marketing ("for Alzheimer's patients") is the single cheapest way to lose the wellness position; the guidance's test turns on claims, so claims are where the discipline goes. "Memory impairment" as a description of the user population is tolerable; "treats", "manages", "monitors" a condition are not.
2. **What "dose text never feeds a dosing feature" buys** (spec L83, L331). The line keeps PAM out of two device categories at once: dosage calculators and dosing-schedule alerting (NXQ), and the broader "patient-specific analysis" that the 2022 guidance treats as device functions. OCR of the label is stored as evidence for identity; nothing computes, schedules, confirms or escalates a dose. The legacy `doses.py` (`check_pills_taken`, `confirm_pills`, streaks, caregiver escalation, [V: code/server/app.py L203-L255; CLAUDE.md L776-L779]) is an adherence tracker; by itself it is Appendix B enforcement discretion, but combined with camera inference about whether a bottle was handled it drifts toward a device-like "did the patient take the dose" claim. ARCH's deletion of `doses` from the memory-layer server (ARCH 2.6) is the right call commercially, not just architecturally; if an adherence feature returns later it must be user-entered, not camera-inferred, and must carry no alert to a professional.
3. **The pilot metric helps.** "Confident-wrong on medication: zero in the pilot" (spec L219) reads to a diligence reviewer as a safety control; keep it in the record.

### 4.2 HIPAA: when a home-care agency is the customer

- Covered entity: "A health care provider who transmits any health information in electronic form in connection with a transaction covered by this subchapter"; business associate: a person who "Creates, receives, maintains, or transmits protected health information for a function or activity regulated by this subchapter"; subcontractors of business associates are business associates; PHI excludes employment records held by the covered entity as employer [V: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-160/subpart-A/section-160.103].
- Consequences: (a) A Medicare-certified home health agency that bills electronically is a covered entity; a private-pay non-medical companion-care agency may not be (it transmits no standard transactions), so the answer is per customer [B on the per-agency fact]. (b) When a covered-entity agency gives PAM client names, medication names (the OCR'd label is "medication" information about an identified person), or receives location reports about a client, PAM is a business associate: BAA with the agency, Security Rule program, breach notification, and BAAs down the chain to Deepgram (its ToS forbids PHI without one), the LLM provider (Anthropic HIPAA readiness exists), Roboflow (Enterprise only), and any labeling vendor. (c) When a family is the customer and the wearer controls the data, HIPAA does not apply, but the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule very likely does: "health care services or supplies" includes "any online services such as a website, mobile application, or internet-connected device that provides mechanisms to track ... medications", and a product "is a personal health record if it can draw any information from multiple sources" [V, secondary: https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2024/07/final-changes-to-health-breach-notification; effective July 29, 2024 per https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/240514-ftc-issues-final-rule-to-expand]. PAM draws from the camera, the wearer and the caregiver, and tracks where medication is. Write the breach-notification plan with the privacy policy, before the pilot.
- Build constraint: keep the agency deployment and the family deployment as two configurations with two data-flow diagrams; the agency one has no third-party service without a BAA, which with whisper offline and no write-path VLM is already almost true.

### 4.3 State recording and consent laws (camera and microphone in a home with aides and visitors)

- Audio is the exposure; the spec's push-to-talk design is the mitigation. Wiretap statutes reach oral communications, not silent video. Massachusetts defines "interception" as "to secretly hear, secretly record, or aid another to secretly hear or secretly record the contents of any wire or oral communication through the use of any intercepting device by any person other than a person given prior authority by all parties to such communication", with criminal penalties and civil liquidated damages of "$100 per day for each day of violation or $1000, whichever is higher" plus punitive damages and fees [V: https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIV/TitleI/Chapter272%20/Section99 §B(4), §C(1), §Q]. California Penal Code 632(a) reaches "a person who, intentionally and without the consent of all parties to a confidential communication, uses an electronic amplifying or recording device to eavesdrop upon or record the confidential communication"; "confidential communication" excludes circumstances "in which the parties to the communication may reasonably expect that the communication may be overheard or recorded" [V: https://california.public.law/codes/penal_code_section_632 §(a), §(c)]. The all-party states for in-person conversations are "California, Delaware, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Montana, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania, and Washington", with Missouri and Oregon all-party for in-person only, Connecticut and Nevada all-party for calls only, Hawaii and Maine all-party in "particularly private places"; and "Each state and the District of Columbia has laws outlawing certain uses of hidden cameras in private places, although many laws are specifically limited to attempts to record nudity" [V: https://www.rcfp.org/introduction-to-reporters-recording-guide/].
- Build constraints, in order of leverage:
  1. **No ambient microphone, ever.** The rig captures video; audio exists only as a push-to-talk clip (`MediaRecorder` started by the wearer's tap) and a caregiver's deliberate voice note. Make that a stated invariant in the spec and the privacy notice ("the microphone is on only while you hold the button"), because it moves the household out of the wiretap statutes for everything except the few seconds of a clip. A push-to-talk clip can still catch an aide speaking in the background; the disclosure and the visible indicator cover that in all-party states, because Massachusetts turns on "secretly" and California on a reasonable expectation of not being recorded.
  2. **Visible indicator, disclosure, one-touch pause** (spec L91) are the consent mechanism for video: a disclosed camera in the home's common areas, with a posted notice and a signed acknowledgement from every regular entrant (aides, family), is the standard the hidden-camera statutes and privacy torts are built around [B as to tort law]. No capture in bathrooms or bedrooms of anyone but the wearer; the mount is on the wearer, so the pause must be reachable by an aide too.
  3. **Aides are someone's employees.** Where the customer is an agency, the agency is the employer and must give its own electronic-monitoring notice where state law requires one (New York's employer notice law is the model) [B]; put it in the agency contract as the agency's obligation.
  4. **Pilot geography.** Run the first households in one-party states or with signed all-party consent in hand; do not pilot in Massachusetts or Illinois without counsel's sign-off on the consent packet (Massachusetts for the "secretly" standard and statutory damages; Illinois for BIPA below).

### 4.4 Biometric laws: what must stay excluded

- Illinois BIPA, as amended by Public Act 103-0769: "Biometric identifier" means "a retina or iris scan, fingerprint, voiceprint, or scan of hand or face geometry", excluding photographs and HIPAA health information; "Biometric information" is "Any information, regardless of how it is captured, converted, stored, or shared, based on an individual's biometric identifier used to identify an individual"; liquidated damages "$1,000" negligent / "$5,000" intentional or reckless, now one award per person for repeated collection by the same method [V: https://ilga.gov/legislation/publicacts/103/103-0769.htm]. Section 15(b)'s written-notice-and-release requirement and 15(a)'s retention schedule were not in the amending Act's text I opened [B on their exact wording].
- Washington RCW 19.375.010: "Biometric identifier" is "Data generated by automatic measurements of an individual's biological characteristics ... that is used to identify a specific individual", and "does not include a physical or digital photograph, video or audio recording or data generated therefrom, or information collected, used, or stored for health care treatment, payment, or operations under [HIPAA]"; "Enroll" means to capture, convert to a template "and store it in a database that matches the biometric identifier to a specific individual" [V: https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Law/RCWArchive/2023/htm/RCW%20%2019%20%20TITLE/RCW%20%2019%20.375%20%20CHAPTER/RCW%20%2019%20.375%20.010.htm]. Texas CUBI is the same family ("record of hand or face geometry") [B].
- What must stay excluded, and why `faces.py` was the right thing to drop:
  1. **Face geometry: none.** `app.py` still exposes `/api/face/save`, `/api/face/who`, `/api/face/sync`, `/api/face/restore` through `face_tools` and `es_faces` ("The ONLY call here that stores biometric", [V: code/server/app.py L559-L606]). Delete the routes and the modules from the memory-layer server (ARCH 2.6 already lists `faces` for deletion). No face detection, no face embedding, no person re-identification embedding, anywhere in the pipeline, including the charger tier. Keyframes containing other people's faces are photographs, which BIPA and RCW 19.375 exclude; they stay subject to the retention policy of spec L91, not to biometric law.
  2. **Hand geometry is the live exposure, and it is in the spec.** MediaPipe returns 21-point hand geometry. Rule 8 uses "hand size" and an appearance bank "seeded" by "an optional 10-second wearer calibration" to decide wearer vs other [V: spec L70]. A stored per-person hand template used to tell the wearer from others is a "scan of hand geometry" used to identify an individual under BIPA's definitions, and an "enrollment" under Washington's. The packet also persists `landmarks[]` for every analysed frame, including aides' hands [V: spec L173; MEMORY B `packet_path`]. Constraints: (a) keep the wearer calibration a per-session, on-device scalar (hand width in pixels at a known distance) plus a small appearance bank for the wearer only, with a written release from the wearer in the consent packet; (b) never build or store a template for anyone who is not the wearer: `other_person` and `unknown` are the only outputs, there is no "which other person"; (c) store landmarks only for the frames that are keyframes and only as long as the localizer needs them, then reduce to `hand_bbox`, `handedness` and the hand-size scalar; (d) in Illinois, Texas and Washington households either obtain written releases from every regular entrant or do not pilot there; (e) record a retention schedule for the wearer's bank (delete on offboarding, and in any case within the BIPA three-year rule [B]).
  3. **Voice: no voiceprint.** STT produces text, not a voiceprint; speaker identification or verification would. Do not add speaker ID to attribute a voice note or a query to a person; the authenticated caregiver session and the push-to-talk tap are the attribution.

### 4.5 The caregiver as a data subject (MEMORY question 3)

- What is stored and spoken: the caregiver's identity (authenticated id, name as the wearer knows them), the time and content of each report, optional photo or voice line, and the attribution in the spoken answer ("your daughter says...") [V: final/MEMORY.md G]. The caregiver is therefore a participant in the research and a user of the product, not a bystander, and needs their own consent: (a) pilot: an IRB-approved secondary-participant consent covering collection of their reports, their voice notes, their photos, storage on the laptop and (if used) transmission to Deepgram/the LLM, retention, the fact that their words are repeated to the wearer by name, and their right to withdraw; (b) product: terms of use plus a privacy notice for the caregiver account with the same elements, and a deletion path that removes the caregiver's identity from past hypotheses while keeping the hypothesis (replace `source = caregiver:<id>` with `caregiver:withdrawn`; the ledger's append-only design handles it as a version bump, not a rewrite).
- Does the bystander audio plan cover a caregiver's voice note? **No, and it does not need to**: a voice note is a one-party recording by the speaker, consented by the act of pressing the button, under the caregiver's own terms. What the bystander plan must still cover is the background of that note (the wearer or an aide audible behind the caregiver) and the clip's retention. Treat caregiver voice notes exactly like wearer push-to-talk clips: transcribe, keep the audio only as long as the correction-loop audit needs it, then delete.
- Spoken-back disclosure has a wearer side too: the wearer with memory impairment hears "your daughter moved your pills." Nothing legal attaches to that, but the consent packet should say that caregiver reports are repeated to the wearer by name, so a caregiver who reports a hidden item (medication locked away) understands the wearer will be told.

### 4.6 Data retention for the correction loop

- Spec L87: corrections logged with source, meaning, timestamp and linkage; a sample human-audited before export as training labels; personalization consent separate from global-training consent. The constraint to add: a retention schedule by data class, written before the pilot and enforced by the ledger's `retention_until` (MEMORY B).
  - Push-to-talk and caregiver audio: transcript kept with the hypothesis; raw audio kept at most 30 days for STT-error audit, then deleted; never leaves the laptop when whisper runs; with Deepgram, `mip_opt_out=true` and Deepgram keeps nothing beyond the request [V: MIP page].
  - Keyframes with other people visible: the L91 policy; a concrete initial value is 30 days for frames with no item evidence, and for evidence frames, as long as the item is durable or until the household offboards, whichever is first [B as a proposal].
  - Training export: only audited samples, only from households that gave the separate global-training consent, de-identified (no caregiver identity, no audio), with a manifest that records the consent version per sample. This is also what makes the export sellable in an acquisition: an acquirer buys data it can prove it may use.
  - Deletion: a household offboarding deletes raw media and audio and leaves the ledger rows that carry no personal content; a caregiver withdrawal is the version bump above.

Numbers that only live in `retention_policy` and never reach a participant are engineering values. **Does BIPA's retention schedule apply to the reduced record or only to the 21-point file?** To the 21-point file for everyone, and to anything derived from it that is used to tell one person from another: the wearer's hand-size scalar and the wearer-only appearance bank are "information ... based on an individual's biometric identifier used to identify an individual" [V: BIPA definitions in PA 103-0769], so the schedule and the written release attach to them; `hand_bbox`, `handedness` and the per-frame presence flag are not measurements of biological characteristics and carry no identification, so they fall outside. The practical consequence is the same as MEMORY's design: one written release for the wearer covering the scalar and the bank, with the retention term in it; nothing derived for anyone else.

**(b) Does hard-coded vs configuration gating of features under the `agency` deployment profile matter for HIPAA business-associate status?** Not for the status itself: PAM is a business associate the moment it creates, receives, maintains or transmits PHI on an agency's behalf [V: 45 CFR 160.103], however the software is built. It matters for three things that follow from the status:

1. **Which subcontractor BAAs are needed.** A business associate must have a BAA with every subcontractor that receives PHI [V: 160.103, business associate (3)(iii)]. If the Deepgram, cloud-LLM and labeling-upload paths cannot execute under `agency` (the code path is absent or fails closed and this is testable), no PHI reaches those vendors and no BAA with them is needed; if the paths exist behind a flag that an installer or a support engineer can flip, counsel will either require the BAAs anyway or require compensating controls, because the risk analysis (45 CFR 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A) [B on the citation]) has to account for the flag being wrong.
2. **What the agency's security questionnaire asks.** "Can PHI egress to a non-BAA vendor be enabled by configuration?" is a standard question; "no, by build" is the answer that closes it. A configuration answer needs the controls below.
3. **What the BAA warrants.** The BAA will warrant that PHI is disclosed only as permitted; a representation that depends on a config value needs an audit trail showing the value never changed.

Recommendation: configuration gating is acceptable, and is what ARCH and MEMORY built (`households.deployment_profile`), provided that (i) the profile is set at household provisioning by the operator, never by the wearer or the caregiver, and `households` is immutable on that column (MEMORY's trigger pattern); (ii) every egress client checks the household's profile at the call site and refuses (fail closed), rather than reading a process-wide feature flag at startup; (iii) a test asserts, under `agency`, zero network egress to any host not on the BAA list (block at the HTTP client, not only at the feature); (iv) profile changes are versioned events in `decisions` with who and when; (v) the `agency` build, when it exists, is a build variant that omits the Deepgram and labeling-upload clients entirely, which turns the configuration answer into the build answer for the questionnaire. Until an agency customer exists, (i)-(iv) are enough and cost a day.

**(c) MEMORY's second file question: is a per-sample manifest enough for `exports`, or must the consent name the data classes?** The consent must name them. A per-sample manifest proves what was exported under which consent version; it cannot create a permission the consent did not give. The Common Rule requires the consent to describe the research and the data, and the broad-consent form for storage and secondary use must describe the types of information that may be used [B on the exact 45 CFR 46.116 wording]. For the pilot's global-training consent, name the classes positively and negatively: may be exported, de-identified: identity crops, foreground masks, reduced hand geometry (bbox and presence flags, not the 21 points), OCR fields with patient name removed, query transcripts with names removed, scoring-sheet rows; never exported: audio, caregiver identity, raw landmarks, the wearer calibration, any frame in which a face is the main subject, and nothing from a household without the flag. Add two sentences the IRB will want: that de-identified samples cannot be withdrawn once pooled, and that the pooled set may be transferred with the business. The `exports` row then records `consent_version` and `data_class`, and the manifest is the audit of a permission the form actually granted.
